Training vs forecast evaluation
In-sample fit vs out-of-sample forecast performance In-sample metrics (RMSE, R², AIC) measure how well a model reproduces the data it was fitted to. A model with more parameters always fits better in-sample — but this does not mean it forecasts better. Overfitting is common: a flexible model learns noise in training data and extrapolates it incorrectly.
The train/test split Fitting on weeks 1–24 and evaluating on weeks 25–28 gives an honest estimate of forecast performance. A model with slightly worse in-sample fit but better out-of-sample RMSE is the preferred choice for forecasting. This is the basis of cross-validation and proper scoring rules like WIS.
If your model has excellent training R² but high forecast RMSE, it is overfitting — too many parameters relative to data length. Try a simpler model (Logistic instead of Richards, or GGM for the growth phase only) or collect more data before the peak. For sub-exponential early growth, GGM or GRM are more appropriate than Exponential or Logistic.
