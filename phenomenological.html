Model hierarchy & parameter reference
Nesting structure Exponential ⊂ GGM (add p) ⊂ GLM (add K) ⊂ GRM (add a) Logistic ⊂ Richards (add a) ⊂ GRM (add p) GLM nests Logistic (p=1) and GGM (K→∞) Gompertz: asymmetric S-curve with fixed inflection at K/e — independent branch The GRM nests all single-wave phenomenological models as special cases.
Parameters defined r = intrinsic growth rate (per time unit). Controls how fast cases multiply early on. K = final epidemic size (carrying capacity). Total cases at saturation. p = scaling of growth (GGM, GLM, GRM). p=1 → exponential early growth; p<1 → sub-exponential (polynomial). Captures spatial heterogeneity and clustering. a = asymmetry (Richards, GRM). a=1 → symmetric (logistic); a<1 → fast rise / slow decline; a>1 → slow rise / fast decline. t_m = inflection time. Time of peak incidence / maximum growth rate. I₀ = initial condition. Cumulative cases at t=0 (for cumulative models) or seed cases (GGM, GLM, GRM).
Phenomenological models describe the shape of an epidemic curve without assuming a transmission mechanism. They are fast to fit, easy to interpret, and well-suited for short-horizon forecasting when only case counts are available. Their main limitation is that parameters have no direct mechanistic meaning — unlike R₀ in compartmental models — and they cannot capture interventions, multiple waves, or population structure without extension.
