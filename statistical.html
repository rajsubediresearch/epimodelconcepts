ARIMA order (p, d, q)
Auto-select fits ARIMA(p,d,q) for all combinations of p ∈ {0–3}, d ∈ {0–2}, q ∈ {0–3} and picks the order with lowest AIC.
p (autoregressive order): How many past values predict the current value. p=1 means today's cases depend on yesterday's. Higher p captures longer memory. Start with p=1 or 2.
d (differencing order): How many times to difference the series to make it stationary. d=0: series is already stationary. d=1: first difference (removes linear trend). d=2: second difference (removes quadratic trend). For epidemic growth, d=1 is typical.
q (moving average order): How many past forecast errors affect the current value. q=1 means today's cases are also influenced by last period's surprise. Helps capture short-term autocorrelation in residuals.
degrees of freedom
Degrees of freedom (df): Controls how wiggly the fitted curve is. Low df (3–4) = smooth, captures broad trend. High df (10+) = flexible, follows local fluctuations but may overfit. Default 6 is a good starting point for epidemic curves spanning 20–50 time points.

Model guide
When to use each model Log-linear, Poisson, NB: best for the exponential growth phase — fast, interpretable, give growth rate and doubling time directly. ARIMA: best when autocorrelation in residuals is present, or when the epidemic has passed the growth phase — captures temporal structure. GAM: best for flexible nonparametric smoothing of the full curve — good for describing shape without assuming a specific functional form.
Comparison strategy For growth phase data: Poisson vs NB (check VMR) vs log-linear are the right comparison. For full curve or post-peak data: ARIMA and GAM will outperform regression models. Always compare both in-sample AIC and out-of-sample forecast RMSE — a model can win on AIC but lose on forecast RMSE if it overfits.
Use "Compare all models" to see the full picture. The best model for forecasting is not always the one with the best in-sample fit.
