Statistical model reference
Count data & distributional assumptions Epidemic case counts are non-negative integers — ordinary linear regression is inappropriate. The choice of error distribution matters: Poisson assumes variance = mean (equidispersion); Negative Binomial allows variance > mean (overdispersion, common in real outbreaks due to clustering and superspreading); Log-linear OLS treats log-transformed counts as approximately normal (quick but ignores count structure).
When to use each model Poisson GLM: early growth phase, counts not overdispersed, baseline for comparison. Negative Binomial GLM: overdispersed counts (variance/mean > 1), superspreading, most real outbreaks. Log-linear OLS: quick exploration, log-linear growth assumption, not for small counts near zero. ARIMA: stationary or differenced series, captures serial autocorrelation, agnostic to mechanism. GAM: non-parametric smooth trend, captures non-linear epidemic shapes without assuming a growth model.
Statistical models describe the data without assuming a transmission mechanism. They excel at short-horizon forecasting when the trend is stable, but they extrapolate the current pattern linearly and cannot capture turning points they haven't seen. Combine with phenomenological or compartmental models for mechanistic insight.
